=== SLIDE 29 ===
9 (vii): The Three Core Concepts
What is an Embedding?
An embedding is a learned, dense vector representation that maps raw data
into a continuous geometric space where semantic relationships are preserved.
Formally:
Semantically similar inputs → nearby vectors
Semantically different inputs → distant vectors
This allows different types of data to be compared and processed in
a unified mathematical space.
Example intuition:
“cat” (text) → vector
image of a cat → vector
sound of a cat → vector
Although these inputs come from different modalities, their embeddings can still be placed close together in the model if they describe the same idea or concept.
When the model learns well, embeddings form an organized “meaning space,” where similar concepts are located near each other, even if they come from different types of data (like text, images, or audio).
29

=== SLIDE 30 ===
9 (vii): The Three Core Concepts
Concept 2: Alignment
What is multimodal alignment?
Multimodal alignment is the process of learning correspondences between elements across different modalities.
It answers:
What in one modality refers to what in another?
Core idea
Alignment establishes cross-modal correspondence at the semantic or temporal level.
Why is alignment challenging?
Alignment is non-trivial (complex, needing reasoning/effort) because modalities differ in:
Representation structure (continuous vs discrete)
Temporal resolution (frame rate vs tokens vs signals)
Spatial structure (pixels vs sequences vs vectors)
Noise and missing information
Level of abstraction (raw signal vs semantic meaning)
Key distinction:
Representation defines what each modality is.
“Alignment defines what corresponds across modalities.”
Example:
Video ↔ Action
Temporal frames correspond to events (e.g., opening a door)
30

=== SLIDE 31 ===
9 (vii): The Three Core Concepts
Concept 3: Fusion
What is multimodal fusion?
Fusion is the process of integrating information from multiple modalities into a unified representation or decision space.
It answers:
How should aligned information be combined for reasoning or prediction?
Types of fusion
Early Fusion (Input-level integration)
Raw or low-level features are combined before deep processing.
Image features + text features → joint input representation
✔ Simple
✖ Limited modality-specific processing
31

=== SLIDE 32 ===
9 (vii): The Three Core Concepts
2. Intermediate Fusion (Representation-level interaction)
Each modality is encoded separately, then combined through interaction layers.
Vision encoder + Language encoder → cross-modal attention / interaction
✔ Most widely used in modern multimodal models
✔ Preserves modality-specific structure
3. Late Fusion (Decision-level integration)
Each modality produces independent predictions, which are then combined.
Vision prediction + Audio prediction → final decision
✔ Robust to missing modalities
✖ Limited deep interaction
Key principle
Fusion is most effective when modalities provide:
Complementary information (different aspects of same event)
Redundant information (confirmation across sources)
Contextual information (one modality disambiguates another)
