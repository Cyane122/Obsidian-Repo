Applications of Attention Mechanisms
Attention mechanisms help a model decide which parts of the input are more important for the current task. The exact meaning of “important” depends on the application.

=== SLIDE 8 ===
What Do We Need Next? (cont…)
8
Key idea: Attention does not simply process everything equally—it assigns more importance to information that is relevant to the current task.
Application | How attention is used | Simple example
Natural Language Processing | Focuses on the most relevant words when understanding or generating text. | Connects important words and context to understand a sentence.
Image Captioning | Focuses on different image regions while generating different words. | Focus on the boy when generating “boy” and the ball when generating “football.”
Speech Recognition | Focuses on relevant parts of the audio signal when producing each word. | Links parts of the speech signal to words such as “open,” “the,” and “door.”
Recommender Systems | Gives more importance to the user's most relevant past behaviors or preferences. | Recent football-related activity can receive more attention when recommending videos.
Video Analysis | Focuses on important frames, objects, or regions instead of treating everything equally. | For detecting a fall, attention can focus on the person's movement across frames.
Medical Diagnosis | Focuses on important regions, features, or clinical information relevant to diagnosis. | A model may give higher attention to a suspicious region in a medical image.
Document Understanding | Focuses on relevant words, tables, images, and their relationships. | Connects “Total amount” with the corresponding value “$1,250” in an invoice.
Robot Manipulation | Focuses on the target object and the relevant part of the scene. | For “Pick up the red cup,” attention focuses on the red cup rather than other objects.

=== SLIDE 13 ===
Why Do We Need Attention ? (cont…)
13
1. Soft Attention (Focus on several parts)
Gives each part of the input an importance score (weight).
Combines information from all parts, giving more importance to the relevant ones.
The model can consider several parts at the same time.
2. Hard Attention (Select specific parts)
Selects one or a few specific parts of the input to focus on.
Gives little or no attention to the unselected parts.
Makes a more selective choice rather than combining information from everywhere.
Example: Understanding a Football Image
Imagine an AI model is asked: "Where is the football?"
Soft vs Hard Attention
Soft Attention
The model assigns different weights to the ball, players, field, and background. It considers information from all these areas, but gives the ball a higher weight.
Hard Attention
The model selects the area containing the ball and focuses on that area, largely ignoring the other parts of the image..

Self Attention (cont…)
21
Q: How Attention Weights Are Calculated
Step 1: Compare the Query with every Key to calculate a relevance score.
Step 2: Scale the scores to keep the calculations numerically stable.
Step 3: Apply Softmax to convert the scores into weights that are positive and add up to 100%.
e.g., Scores [2.1, 0.4, 1.0] → Weights [71%, 9%, 20%]
Step 4: Multiply each Value by its corresponding weight.
A higher weight gives more influence to that Value.
Step 5: Add the weighted Values together to produce the final attention output.
Attention Scores & Weights
Example
Suppose we have three Values:
V₁, V₂, V₃
and their attention weights are:
71%, 9%, 20%
Then:
Attention Output = (0.71 × V₁) + (0.09 × V₂) + (0.20 × V₃)
i.e.,
V1 = Boy
V2 = Football
V3 = Football Ground
Q and K determine relevance
Weights determine importance
V provides the information

Self-Attention vs. Cross-Attention
Self-Attention | Look within yourself.
One input looks at other parts of the same input to understand their relationships.
Cross-Attention | Look at another input.
One input looks at another input to find the information it needs.
Technical
Difference | Both follow the same basic process:
Q + K → Scores → Weights → Values → Output
The main difference is where Q, K, and V come from.
Self-Attention | Q, K, and V come from the same input.
Example: Boy ↔ Football  = the boy is playing with the football.
Boy ↔ Field = the boy is on the field.
Cross-Attention | Q comes from one input, while K and V come from another input.
Example: Text + Image
The text provides the Query, while the image provides the Keys and Values.

5. From Attention to Transformers
31
Positional Encoding
What Is Positional Encoding?
Positional encoding describes the location or position of an entity in a sequence
Each position is assigned a unique representation.
This helps the Transformer model understand the order and location of elements in the input.
Position is important because the same elements can have different meanings when their positions change.
e.g., “A car is in front of the tree.” and “A tree is in front of the car.”
Attention tells the model what is related
Positional Encoding tells it where each element is and helps distinguish meaning based on position.

This makes Transformers faster and more efficient.

From Attention to Transformers (cont…)

Transformer Architecture
A Transformer is built by stacking multiple Encoder and Decoder blocks.
Encoder
Input Embedding: converts input tokens into numerical vectors.
Positional Encoding: adds information about the position of each token.
Multi-Head Attention: captures different relationships between input tokens.
Feed-Forward Network: further processes the attended information.
Residual (Add) + Normalization (Norm): help preserve information and keep values stable across layers.
Decoder
Output Embedding: converts output tokens into vectors; during training, the target sequence is shifted right.
Masked Multi-Head Attention: lets each position attend only to earlier tokens, preventing access to future tokens.
Cross-Attention: connects the Decoder to the Encoder's output and focuses on relevant input information.
Feed-Forward Network: further transforms the information.
Residual (Add) + Normalization (Norm): help information flow through the layers.
Output
Linear: projects the Decoder output to the size of the vocabulary.
Softmax: converts the values into probabilities for the next token.

=== SLIDE 36 ===
6. Transformers across Modalities
36
Text → Tokens
Before a Transformer can process text, the text is split into smaller units called tokens. These can be whole words or parts of words.
e.g., “unbelievable” may be split into “un” + “believe” + “able.”
Each token is converted into an embedding layer.
Positional encoding for the position of each token in the sequence.
The result is a sequence of vectors containing both meaning and position, which is then given to the Transformer.
Text → Tokens → Embeddings + Positional Encoding → Transformer

=== SLIDE 37 ===
Transformers across Modalities (cont…)
37
Images → Patches
The image is divided into small, fixed-size patches.
e.g., a 224 × 224 image divided into 16 × 16 patches produces a 14 × 14 grid, giving 196 patches.
Each patch is flattened and converted into an embedding vector.
Positional encoding is added so the model knows where each patch is located in the image.
The result is a sequence of patch embeddings, similar to a sequence of word embeddings in text.
Image → Patches → Patch Embeddings + Positional Encoding → Transformer

=== SLIDE 38 ===
Transformers across Modalities (cont…)
38
Audio → Segments
Audio is a continuous waveform, so it is divided into short time segments, often called frames.
Each segment is converted into features, such as a spectrogram, and then into an embedding vector.
Positional encoding is added so the model knows the order of the segments in time.
The result is a sequence of audio embeddings that can be processed by the Transformer.
Example: The 5th segment keeps its position, so the model knows when it occurs.
Audio → Segments → Audio Embeddings + Positional Encoding → Transformer

=== SLIDE 39 ===
Transformers across Modalities (cont…)
39
A Common Attention-Based Architecture Across Inputs
Text, images, and audio are converted into different types of input units.
These units are converted into embeddings, giving each modality a common vector-based representation.
Once represented as embeddings, they can be processed by a Transformer architecture.
The Transformer uses core components such as self-attention, multi-head attention, feed-forward layers, and normalization.
The main difference is how each modality is converted into tokens or segments before entering the Transformer.
Different inputs → Different representations → Embeddings → Attention-Based Transformer

7. Transformers for Multimodal AI
42
Vision + Language
Images and text are converted into embeddings.
Cross-attention connects the two modalities, allowing information from one to influence the other.
Example: “What color is the car?” → the text focuses on the relevant image region → “Red.”
This supports tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering, and image–text retrieval.
During training, paired images and text help the model learn relationships between visual content and language.
Vision provides visual information + Language provides meaning → Cross-Attention connects them.

=== SLIDE 43 ===
Transformers for Multimodal AI (cont…)
43
Audio + Language
Audio is divided into short segments and converted into audio embeddings, while text is represented as word or sub-word tokens.
Cross-attention connects the two modalities, allowing language to use relevant information from the audio.
Example: A spoken lecture → the model focuses on relevant audio segments → written transcript.
This supports tasks such as speech recognition, speech translation, and audio captioning.
The same approach can work in the opposite direction, where text guides speech generation.
Audio provides spoken information + Language provides meaning → Cross-Attention connects them.

=== SLIDE 44 ===
Transformers for Multimodal AI (cont…)
44
Video + Language
Video contains visual information and time. Each frame can be divided into patches and converted into embeddings.
The model needs to understand where something appears and when it happens.
Cross-attention connects language with video, allowing a text query to focus on relevant frames and regions.
Example: “When does the person throw?” → the model focuses on the relevant frames → identifies the action.
This supports tasks such as video captioning, video question answering, and video search.
Video provides visual information over time + Language describes what to look for → Cross-Attention connects them.

Practical Limitations
Large Data: Training needs large amounts of well-aligned multimodal data.
High Computation: Attention can be expensive for long sequences, especially video.
Data Quality: Poor data can lead to weak connections between modalities.
Preprocessing: Different modalities require different preprocessing before entering the Transformer.
Multimodal Transformers are powerful, but they require large, high-quality datasets, substantial computational resources, and modality-specific preprocessing.
