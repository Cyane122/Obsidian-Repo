1. Motivation
According to the WHO, worldwide, approximately 1.19 million people die each year as a result of road traffic crashes [1].
In the US, nationwide, each year, over 38,700 vehicle crashes occur in fog, where around 21% of these are weather-related [2,3].
In 2015 in Korea, the fatal accident rate was 6.9% on foggy days, while on clear days it was 2.5% [4].
Challenges in Vision
Image degradation from haze and low-light causes failures in object detection, navigation, etc.
Autonomous systems (e.g., self-driving cars, surveillance) demand clear vision for accurate navigation.
Weather Impact on Road Safety
Problem Statement
Most existing methods assume a single haze type or work on synthetic data.
Lack of large-scale, diverse datasets.

5. Conclusion
Limitations
Our proposed haze-aware dehazing framework relies on a pre-trained classifier
Wrong classification may lead to inaccurate dehazing
Training data are synthetic and may not cover the real-world complexity
Future Scopes
Developing a unified method for dehazing multiple haze types
Developing models with less computational expense.
Developed robust pipelines for image enhancement under real atmospheric degradation.
Contributed two high-quality large datasets.
Proposed adaptive solutions improving both image quality and downstream vision tasks.
